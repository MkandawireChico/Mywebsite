CASE STUDY 03 · HEALTH OPERATIONS

Hospital Patient Flow & Workforce Planning

Finding where waiting accumulates and exploring operational responses with transparent assumptions.

Open live application ↗View source repository ↗

THE QUESTION

Where do patients lose time in the hospital pathway, which groups show the strongest delay signals, and how might managers investigate improvements?

A patient-flow analytics command centre combining data-quality controls, waiting-time analysis, scenario comparisons, a long-wait baseline model and workforce planning in MWK.

01 / CONTEXT

Why this problem matters.

Patient journeys pass through connected stages such as registration, triage, consultation, laboratory and pharmacy. Delays can accumulate at different points. This case study makes those patterns measurable in a Malawi-inspired district-hospital setting and keeps measurement, diagnosis and scenario exploration separate.

02 / APPROACH

How the work is structured.

01

Prepare and audit

The workflow removes exact duplicates, standardises department aliases, flags invalid timestamp sequences and negative waits, and preserves an audit of excluded records.

02

Measure patient flow

It derives arrival-hour, peak-period and long-wait features, then reports mean, median, P90, long-wait share, stage and department summaries, hourly patterns and priority comparisons.

03

Explore scenarios

Two consultation-wait reduction scenarios are compared with the baseline on the same valid analysis cohort. Scenario assumptions are explicit and outputs are not presented as causal estimates.

04

Support planning

The Streamlit command centre includes a workforce coverage view, capacity sensitivity and a role-based staffing budget planner with user-entered assumptions in MWK.

WHAT THE ANALYSIS SHOWS

Laboratory stage-wait P90 was 182.9 minutes and laboratory contributed 68.7% of summed observed stage-wait minutes. Outpatient Department had the highest department P90 total wait at 73.5 minutes. The Routine consultation-reduction scenario lowered P90 from 48.2 to 41.4 minutes and reduced the share above 60 minutes by 2.10 percentage points.

04 / PRACTICAL RELEVANCE

How the output can be used.

The dashboard helps an operations team move from a headline wait metric to a more targeted investigation: compare departments with volume context, inspect time-of-day patterns, review stage-level upper tails and explore clearly labelled what-if scenarios. The MWK planner allows local cost assumptions to be entered rather than inventing salary benchmarks.

05 / TECHNOLOGY

Tools and implementation.

Python · Pandas · NumPy · Streamlit · Plotly · Scikit-learn · Pytest · Ruff

SCOPE & LIMITATIONS

Interpreting the results.

The analysis explores waiting times and operational bottlenecks using generated encounter records. Its findings illustrate analytical methods rather than actual hospital performance.
